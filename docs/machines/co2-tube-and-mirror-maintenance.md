# CO₂ tube and mirror maintenance

Glass-tube CO₂ machines earn their keep when cooling stays stable and the beam path stays clean and centered. Most “my laser got weak overnight” stories in the community turn out to be dirty optics, loose lens rings, drifting alignment, or a tube that was cooked by bad cooling — not mysterious software.

## Cooling habits that protect the tube

Community guidance (LightBurn forum and Sawmill Creek) converges on a few practical rules:

- Keep coolant **stable**, not just “cold.” Large swings (bucket + ice) show up as power drift and uneven engraves.
- Many operators aim near **18–20 °C** water temperature for everyday work. Running much hotter tends to drop output and age tubes faster; running far below ambient dew point can invite **condensation** on optics and high-voltage parts.
- Watch for **flow**, not only temperature: bubbles, kinked lines, failed pumps, and ignored water-protect inputs are classic tube killers.
- Freezing shops need circulating coolant and freeze protection per the tube maker — cracked glass tubes from frozen water are a known risk.

!!! tip "Consistency beats heroics"
    Engraving large fills with ± a couple of degrees of water swing can band. Stabilize temperature before you chase new LightBurn settings.

## Optics cleaning (mirrors and lens)

Smoke and resin land on mirrors and the focus lens. How often you clean depends on extraction quality and the materials you run — some shops go weeks between cleanings with strong air assist and exhaust; heavy smoke jobs dirty optics much faster.

Practical consensus from LightBurn forum cleaning threads:

- Wear gloves — skin oils on a lens are an expensive fingerprint.
- **Isopropyl alcohol** on proper optics wipes/tissues is the usual first tool for glass/molybdenum optics.
- Treat **gold-plated silicon** mirrors gently — they scratch and pit more easily than molybdenum “slugs.”
- Confirm the focus lens is seated correctly after cleaning (convex orientation and lock ring per your head design — follow the machine manual).
- Clean the **nozzle tip** too; char on the tip is normal, but it should not stay forever.

!!! warning "Do not polish with random shop rags"
    Grit turns a cleaning session into a new lens purchase. Use optics-rated wipes.

## Alignment is maintenance

Align from the tube toward the lens, one station at a time, with **low-power pulses** on targets. Center the burn at both near and far travel for each mirror. Community advice repeatedly warns:

- Blasting high power through tape destroys the evidence you need.
- Loose lock rings on mirrors/lens look like “sudden power loss.”
- After shipping, crashes, tube swaps, or corner-to-corner cut differences, plan an alignment check.

We will not publish a one-size pulse recipe here — use your manufacturer sequence and wavelength-correct eyewear. For the broader picture see [focus and alignment](focus-and-alignment.md).

## Quick monthly checklist

| Check | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Coolant level, temp setpoint, flow | Protects the tube |
| Lens + M2/M3 cleanliness (flashlight) | Restores “lost” power |
| Lock rings snug | Stops intermittent weak cuts |
| Soft corner cut test | Catches alignment drift early |
| Exhaust / air assist working | Optics stay cleaner longer |
