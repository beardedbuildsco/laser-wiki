# Laser eye safety and fire

Lasers injure eyes, start fires, and make toxic smoke. This page expands the practical rules we already stress in [ventilation and safety](../machines/ventilation-and-safety.md) — especially **eyewear wavelength matching** and **fire discipline** — for CO₂, fiber, and UV shops.

## Eyewear: wavelength first, tint second

| Source (typical) | Ballpark wavelength | Eyewear must match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CO₂ | ~10,600 nm | CO₂-rated OD for that band |
| Fiber | ~1064 nm | NIR/fiber-rated — not CO₂ glasses |
| UV galvo | often ~355 nm | UV-rated for that band |

Dark green plastic that “came in the box” is not automatically correct. Check the marked wavelength range and optical density (OD) against your laser. Enclosed Class 1-style cabinets with certified windows still deserve respect — do not defeat interlocks.

!!! danger "Reflections count"
    Class 4 accessible beams can injure via specular reflection. Jewelry, unfinished metal, and open doors are not fashion choices during a job.

## Fire

Organic cutting (wood, paper, acrylic) is where most shop fires start. Community safety writing and makerspace practice agree:

- Never leave a cutting job unattended
- Keep a suitable extinguisher within reach; know whether to kill power first
- Clean beds so scrap is not tinder
- Use air assist where it reduces flare-ups
- Know what you are cutting — PVC/vinyl remain hard stops ([what you can laser](../materials/what-you-can-laser.md))

Fiber marking on bare metal is lower fire risk than CO₂ wood cutting, but coatings, fixtures, and paper jigs still burn. UV glass work still needs the same “stay present” habit when organics are in the field.

## Fumes (short reminder)

Eyewear does not filter lungs. Exhaust outdoors or use a filter system maintained on schedule. Acrylic, MDF, and coated metals all produce junk you should not casually breathe.


## Training and access control

- Brief anyone who “just wants to try a keychain”
- Post wavelength/OD requirements on the enclosure
- Keep spare correct eyewear, not a junk drawer of unknown glasses
- Log near-misses (flare-ups, defeated interlocks) so habits improve

We are a shop sharing practical norms — not your laser safety officer. When in doubt, hire a qualified review for your facility.
