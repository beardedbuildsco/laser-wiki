This guide uses broad tiers and use cases. We deliberately avoid quoting street prices or naming specific models we have not verified for you — those change fast. Use this to shortlist a *type* and a *capability band*, then compare current listings yourself.

## Before you spend a dollar

Answer these in writing:

1. **Primary materials** (wood, acrylic, coated tumblers, bare metal, leather…)
2. **Largest piece** you expect to engrave or cut regularly
3. **Where the machine lives** (garage, spare room, shared shop) and how you will **vent fumes**
4. **Software** you want (many hobby machines work with LightBurn — confirm controller compatibility)
5. **Budget that includes extras**: exhaust, air assist, honeycomb, fire extinguisher, materials for testing, rotary if needed

If ventilation is an unsolved problem, pause the purchase. A laser without safe exhaust is a liability, not an asset. See [ventilation and safety](../machines/ventilation-and-safety.md).

## Match the machine family first

| If most of your work is… | Lean toward… | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wood signs, slate, leather, paper, coated metals | Diode or CO₂ | Strong on organics; diodes often cheaper to enter |
| Clean acrylic cuts and thicker organics | CO₂ | Wavelength suited to acrylic/wood cutting |
| Bare metal logos, serials, deep metal marks | Fiber (often galvo) | Metal absorption at fiber wavelengths |
| High-volume identical small marks | Galvo fiber | Speed inside a fixed field |

Details: [laser types](../machines/laser-types.md).

## Budget tiers (capability bands)

Prices move; treat these as **capability bands**, not quotes.

### Entry / learning tier

**Goal:** learn LightBurn, finish small gifts, validate that you enjoy the workflow.

Typical traits:

- Smaller work area
- Lower continuous power (enough for engraving and light cutting on thin materials)
- May be open-frame or lightly enclosed — plan eyewear and enclosure upgrades seriously
- Basic air assist or add-on needed

**Good fit:** hobbyists, content creators testing product ideas, side hustles with low volume.

**Watch-outs:** undersized exhaust, missing fire safety plan, buying only on “watts” without checking real cut tests from trusted reviewers.

### Small-shop / side-business tier

**Goal:** repeatable products (plaques, tumblers, small batch signs) with less fighting the machine.

Typical traits:

- Enclosed cabinet or well-designed enclosure
- Stronger exhaust path and air assist
- Bed size that fits your best-selling blank
- Reliable controller that LightBurn supports
- Room in the budget for a rotary if tumblers matter

**Good fit:** Etsy/Shopify makers, plaque shops, event vendors.

**Watch-outs:** ignoring maintenance (rails, lenses, belts), skipping test grids, promising metal cutting on a machine that only marks coatings.

### Production / specialized tier

**Goal:** speed, uptime, and a narrow material focus done well.

Typical traits:

- Higher power or faster marking architecture (e.g. galvo fiber for metal)
- Redundant safety features, better fume extraction
- Fixturing, jigs, and process documentation
- Possibly a second machine for a second material family

**Good fit:** shops with steady B2B orders, nameplates, serialized parts.

**Watch-outs:** buying production gear before you have demand; skipping operator training.

## Use-case playbooks

### Custom plaques and awards

Priorities: flat bed large enough for your blanks, clean engraving on wood/acrylic/slate/metal-look laminates, predictable focus. Many plaque workflows succeed on a capable diode or CO₂. Metal deep-engrave nameplates may want fiber.

Walkthrough mindset: [engraving a plaque](../projects/engraving-a-plaque.md).

### Tumblers and drinkware

Priorities: a **rotary** that your controller supports, consistent diameter settings in LightBurn, coatings that engrave cleanly. Confirm rotary compatibility before you buy the machine.

See [rotary engraving](../projects/rotary-engraving.md).

### Acrylic products

Priorities: CO₂ is usually the cleaner cutting choice for clear acrylic. Test flame-polished vs sanded edges for your brand. Ventilation is non-negotiable — acrylic fumes need proper exhaust.

### Metal marking

Priorities: fiber marking systems; understand field size on galvo units; fixture parts so every mark lands in focus.

## Software and ecosystem

- Prefer controllers with **known LightBurn support** if that is how you want to work.
- Factor the LightBurn license into your budget if your machine does not bundle it.
- Export/import habits (SVG, DXF) matter as much as raw laser power when clients send artwork.

## Safety and compliance checklist (non-optional)

- Correct **laser safety eyewear** for your wavelength (or a certified enclosure that makes open-beam eyewear rules clear for your class of device)
- **Fire** plan: never leave a cutting job unattended; keep a suitable extinguisher rated for the materials you cut
- **Exhaust** to the outdoors or a filter system rated for your process — know what your local rules expect
- Read the manual for interlocks, emergency stop, and maintenance

We are not your compliance officer — verify regulations for your location.

## A simple decision script

```text
1. List top 3 products you will sell in 90 days
2. Map each product → material → machine family
3. If two families appear, plan which machine comes first (revenue first)
4. Size the bed to your largest common blank + margin
5. Price the *whole* setup (machine + vent + safety + software + rotary)
6. Only then compare specific models currently for sale
```
