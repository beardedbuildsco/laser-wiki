# Finding customers for your laser business

The machine is not the business. Customers are. This page is a practical map of **who buys laser work**, **where to meet them**, and **how to pitch** — so you stop waiting for “someone to find your Etsy shop” and start building a pipeline that fits *your* lane.

## Pick your lane first

You cannot market to everyone at once. A realtor who wants 50 branded tumblers is a different sale than an Etsy buyer hunting a pet memorial, and both are different from a shop that needs asset tags every quarter.

Before you print business cards or cold-message fifty Facebook groups:

1. **Name 1–2 customer types** you can serve well with the machine and blanks you already have
2. **Name one primary channel** (door-to-door samples, craft fairs, Google Business, wholesale, marketplace — pick a main, not seven)
3. **Define a simple offer** (e.g. “custom drinkware for local businesses” or “employee awards + plaques”) so your pitch is one sentence

Depth beats scatter. You can add a second lane after the first one is paying.

## Customer types at a glance

Use this as a menu, not a to-do list. Steal the rows that match your gear and personality.

| Customer type | What they often buy | Where to find them | Pitch angle | Price / volume pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Local businesses / B2B** (restaurants, realtors, contractors, dealerships, breweries, salons) | Branded tumblers, cutting boards, keychains, coasters, signage, closing gifts | Walk-ins, Chamber events, Main Street, LinkedIn, Google Maps list of nearby shops | “Make your brand something people keep on their desk / in their hand” | Mid ticket; repeat if you become their go-to gift vendor |
| **Corporate gifts & employee awards** | Plaques, acrylic awards, etched glass, branded merch batches | HR / office managers, LinkedIn, local companies, referral from one happy office | Recognition that looks premium without a big awards-house lead time | Higher ticket; batch jobs; proof rounds matter |
| **Schools, teams, clubs** | Spirit boards, senior gifts, fundraiser items, coach plaques, team drinkware | Boosters, athletic directors, PTA, team parents, coaches | Fundraiser-friendly pricing + easy reorders each season | Volume with tight margins unless you productize |
| **Events & weddings** | Place cards, favors, guestbook alternatives, seating charts, date-night gifts | Planners, venues, bridal Facebook groups (follow rules), word of mouth | Deadline-proof process + mockups; you sell calm as much as product | Spike around dates; deposit + rush fees |
| **Retail consumers** (Etsy, Shopify, Amazon Handmade, your site) | Personalized gifts, home décor, pet items, name signs | Marketplaces, SEO/social, ads if budget allows | Photos, reviews, fast ship, clear personalization fields | Many small orders; fees eat margin — price for that |
| **Craft fairs & markets** | Ready-to-buy inventory + custom-on-the-spot (names, dates) | Municipal markets, church bazaars, seasonal festivals | Stand-out product + business card / QR to social or shop | Cash-day sales + custom orders to fill the week after |
| **Wholesale to boutiques / gift shops** | Curated SKUs at wholesale, sometimes consignment | Local gift shops, buyer email, markets that allow wholesale talk | Reliable restocks, simple line sheet, net terms only when you can afford them | Lower per-unit; higher volume; protect wholesale vs retail |
| **Memorials & pets** | Photo frames, urn plates, collar tags, slate/wood memorials | Vets (carefully), pet groomers, funeral homes (relationship-based), social | Empathy, clear proofs, no hard sell; referral cards in the box | Emotional buy; reputation-driven; referrals compound |
| **First responders & military** | Unit gifts, retirement plaques, thin-line items, challenge-adjacent merch | Stations (with permission), base thrift/gift channels where allowed, spouse groups | Respect + accurate insignia; ask before assuming logos are free to use | Loyal once trust is earned; watch trademark / official marks |
| **Industrial / shop marking** (often **fiber**) | Asset tags, nameplates, serial plates, tool marking, part IDs | Machine shops, fabricators, warehouses, maintenance managers | Durability, consistency, and turnaround vs sending parts out | Recurring B2B; quote by setup + per-piece; see [fiber basics](../machines/fiber-laser-basics-and-marking.md) |

If you only have a CO₂ gantry, lean hard into wood, acrylic, tumblers (with rotary), leather, and glass-adjacent work. If you have fiber, industrial nameplates and metal gifts open doors CO₂ cannot.

## Strategies that actually fill the calendar

### Door-to-door with a sample kit

Still one of the highest-signal moves for local B2B:

- Build a **small kit**: 3–5 finished pieces (tumbler, keychain, coaster, mini plaque) plus a one-pager with your phone, Instagram/Facebook, and QR to a simple order form or email
- Target **10–20 nearby businesses** in one afternoon — restaurants, realtors, breweries, salons, dealerships
- Lead with the sample in hand: “We laser custom gifts for local shops — thought you’d want to see one with a real finish, not a phone photo”
- Leave the piece or the card; do **not** pressure for a same-day order
- Follow up once (text or email) a few days later

A physical sample beats a cold DM nine times out of ten.

### Sample-driven outreach (logo on one piece)

Ask for a vector or high-res logo, engrave **one** complimentary or deeply discounted sample on a product they already use (their cup style, their wood tone). Put your mark discreetly on the bottom or include a card. Goal: get the sample on a manager’s desk so reorders are easy.

Cap free samples (time and blanks are money). Track who received what.

### Referral and repeat programs

- After a happy order: “If you send a business that books, I’ll knock $X off your next order” (or a free upgrade — pick one rule and stick to it)
- For offices and teams: keep a simple reorder sheet (same plaque size, same tumbler SKU) so the second order is a reply, not a redesign
- Ask for a Google review when the timing feels right — local SEO compounds

### Local Facebook groups & Marketplace

- Read group rules before posting; many ban overt selling — lead with value (“local laser shop — happy to quote school fundraiser drinkware”) or use allowed promo threads
- Marketplace works for ready inventory and “custom available” listings with clear photos and turnaround times
- Answer comments fast; treat every inquiry like a lead, even the tire-kickers

### Instagram, TikTok, YouTube — process content

Show the work: masking, focus, the first pass, the reveal. Short process clips sell capacity better than static product grids. Point every video to the same clear CTA (DM “PLAQUE”, link in bio, or course/shop as fits the video). Consistency beats virality you cannot control.

### Google Business Profile & local SEO

If you take local pickup or serve a city:

- Claim and complete a [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) (or current Google equivalent)
- Real photos of finished work, hours, service area, and categories that match what you sell
- Ask happy local customers for reviews
- A simple website or even a well-kept Facebook Page with NAP (name, address, phone) consistency helps you show up when someone searches “custom laser engraving near me”

### Etsy / Shopify / your own store

Marketplaces bring traffic; they also take fees and train buyers to compare on price. Productize (clear titles, mockups, personalization fields), photograph on real backgrounds, and price using your [cost stack](pricing-laser-work.md) after fees. Your own Shopify or site is slower to fill but you own the customer list.

### Craft fairs and markets

Bring:

- A **tight** inventory of proven sellers (not every blank you own)
- A custom station if your process allows (name/date on a standard blank) — collect payment and email for pickup/ship
- A stack of cards or a QR to Instagram / shop / email list
- Cash + card reader; clear prices on every piece
- A notebook or form for custom quotes you will not finish at the booth

Goal for the booth is twofold: same-day cash **and** a list of people to fulfill and follow up with next week.

### Wholesale basics

- Wholesale is usually a **share of retail** (common starting conversations land around half of your retail — set your retail high enough first)
- Line sheet: SKU, photo, wholesale price, case pack, lead time
- Do not undercut your wholesale partners on your public site without a clear MAP-style rule you can keep
- Start with one or two shops you can restock reliably

### Email list

Every fair, every package insert, every Instagram bio should offer a reason to join a list (new product drops, seasonal gift guides, “first look at holiday blanks”). Own the list so algorithm changes do not erase your pipeline.

## Simple pitch templates

Adapt the tone; keep them short.

### Walk-in / door pitch (30 seconds)

> Hi — I’m with [Shop Name]. We laser custom gifts and signage for local businesses. I brought a sample so you can feel the finish. If branded tumblers, plaques, or closing gifts ever come up, I’d love to be the local shop you text. Here’s my card / QR — happy to put your logo on one piece so you can see it in person.

### Short DM (Facebook / Instagram)

> Hey [Name] — local laser shop here. Saw [their business / post]. I make custom [tumblers / plaques / signs] for shops like yours. Would you want a quick quote, or I can put your logo on one sample so you can see quality before committing?

### Email to a business

> Subject: Custom laser gifts for [Business Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I’m [You] at [Shop]. We engrave and cut custom pieces for local businesses — drinkware, plaques, and small branded gifts.
>
> Happy to send a one-page price sheet for common items, or engrave a single sample with your logo so you can judge the finish in person.
>
> Turnaround on most standard blanks is about [X] business days after proof approval.
>
> Thanks for reading —  
> [Name] · [Phone] · [Link]

### Craft-fair follow-up text

> Thanks for stopping by the booth Saturday — here’s the link to finish your custom [item] order: [link]. Reply with the exact name/date spelling and I’ll send a proof before I fire the laser.

## Pricing and quotes

Marketing fills the inbox; pricing keeps the lights on. Build every quote from materials, labor, machine time, overhead, profit, and fees — see **[Pricing laser work](pricing-laser-work.md)**. For B2B and events, always include:

- What is in the price (and what is not)
- Proof rounds included
- Deposit / cancellation rule
- Rush fee if the date is compressed
- Sales tax handling (see below)

Do not apologize for professional pricing. A clear “no” on a bad-fit budget is better than a job that teaches you to hate the work.

## The boring (required) stuff

None of this is legal or tax advice — rules vary by city and state. Before you take money regularly:

- **Business license / DBA** as your locality requires
- **Sales tax permit** if your state taxes tangible goods (many laser products are taxable; marketplace facilitators sometimes collect for you — verify for *your* channels)
- **Income tax**: track income and expenses from day one; talk to a tax pro about estimated payments and whether an LLC or similar structure fits you
- **Insurance** once you leave hobby volume (general liability is a common starting conversation)
- **Logo / trademark**: get permission for brand marks; schools and agencies often have brand guides

When in doubt: **check your state department of revenue / local business office, and talk to a tax professional.** This wiki will not tell you your rate or your filing calendar.

## 30-day starter plan

A simple checklist if you are starting from near zero local demand. Adjust dates to your life; keep the sequence.

**Week 1 — Lane and offer**

- [ ] Pick one primary customer type and one backup
- [ ] Write a one-sentence offer and a three-item starter menu with prices from your [pricing method](pricing-laser-work.md)
- [ ] Photograph 5–10 best pieces in good light (phone is fine)
- [ ] Set up or clean up: Google Business *or* Instagram/Facebook *or* Etsy — one primary storefront, not five half-built ones
- [ ] Confirm sales-tax / license basics for your area (or book the tax-pro call)

**Week 2 — Samples and local presence**

- [ ] Build a sample kit (3–5 pieces + leave-behind card with QR)
- [ ] Visit 10 local businesses with the kit; log every visit
- [ ] Post 3 process or finished-work pieces on your primary social channel
- [ ] Ask 2 happy past buyers (friends count at the start) for a review or referral

**Week 3 — Conversations and one event path**

- [ ] Follow up every Week-2 visit once
- [ ] Send 5 personalized DMs or emails using the templates above
- [ ] Apply or book **one** craft fair / market **or** one Chamber / networking event in the next 60 days
- [ ] Start a simple email or text list (even a Google Form is enough)

**Week 4 — Productize and measure**

- [ ] Turn the most-requested item into a repeatable SKU (same blank, same price, clear personalization rules)
- [ ] Quote at least 3 real inquiries with deposits or clear next steps
- [ ] Review: which channel got replies? Double down next month; pause the dead ones
- [ ] Block time on the calendar for marketing every week — customer work does not replace pipeline work
