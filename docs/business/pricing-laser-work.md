# Pricing laser work

Underpricing is how laser hobbies stay hobbies forever. This page is a **method** for building prices — not a list of “the market rate” numbers. Markets differ by city, niche, and whether you sell on Etsy, Shopify, wholesale, or local pickup.

## What you are really selling

Customers pay for:

- Design and file prep (often the largest hidden cost)
- Machine time and consumables
- Materials and finishing
- Packaging and shipping labor
- Overhead (rent, electricity, software, marketing, broken tubes, failed blanks)
- Profit (the point)

If your price only covers “a few minutes of laser time,” you are donating design work.

## A simple cost stack

```text
Price ≈ Materials + Labor + Machine + Overhead share + Profit + Fees
```

Walk each term honestly.

### Materials

Blank cost, waste factor (test scraps, misgraves), adhesives, finishes, hardware, packaging. Track actual invoice costs, not hopes.

### Labor

Include:

- Email / phone time to gather text and approvals
- Artwork cleanup and proofing
- Machine setup, focus, fixturing
- Run time (you are present for fire watch — that is labor)
- Finishing, QC, packing
- Remakes (budget a percentage; they happen)

Pay yourself a real hourly rate in the model even if you “do not take a wage yet.” Otherwise the business cannot grow into hiring help.

### Machine

Think in **burdened machine time**: purchase price amortized over useful life, maintenance, optics, electricity, and the fact that the machine cannot take every job at once. You do not need perfect accounting software on day one — you need a non-zero machine rate so free jobs do not feel free.

### Overhead share

Website fees, LightBurn, insurance, marketplace commissions, advertising. Allocate a percentage on top of direct costs or fold into hourly rates — pick one system and use it consistently.

### Profit and fees

After costs, add margin. Then add payment processing and platform fees *on top of* or *grossed into* the price so they do not erase margin.

## Pricing models that work in practice

| Model | Best for | Watch-out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cost-plus** | Custom one-offs | Underestimating design hours |
| **Productized menu** | Repeat SKUs (standard plaque sizes) | Updating when material costs jump |
| **Minimum job fee** | Tiny jobs | Clients pushing “just a quick name” forever |
| **Quantity breaks** | Batches with shared setup | Setup still exists — do not give it away at qty 2 |
| **Rush fee** | Deadline compression | Must be large enough to actually deprioritize other work |

## Variable-data jobs (many unique names)

A hundred plaques with different names is not the same as one plaque times a hundred. File prep, proofing, and QC scale hard. Price:

- A setup / templating fee
- A per-unit run fee
- Proof rounds (include one round; charge for endless revisions)

## Quotes without guessing market numbers

When someone asks “what do you charge for plaques?”:

1. Gather size, material, quantity, deadline, art status
2. Estimate minutes for prep + run + finish
3. Run your cost stack
4. Present a clear quote with validity date and proof policy
5. Do not apologize for professional pricing

If the customer’s budget only supports DIY, that can be a kind referral toward learning resources — not a reason to destroy your margin.

## Discounts and “exposure”

- Friends-and-family discounts should be intentional and capped
- Charity jobs: decide annual capacity in advance
- “Exposure” does not pay for tubes or rent

## Review prices on a schedule

Revisit menu prices when:

- Blank suppliers raise costs
- Your scrap rate changes
- Demand exceeds hours in the week (raise prices or lengthen lead time)
- You add a faster machine (recalculate — do not auto-race to the bottom)
