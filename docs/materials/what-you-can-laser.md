# What you can (and cannot) laser

Lasers are selective. A material that looks “plasticky” might cut beautifully — or release chlorine gas. This page is a shop-floor map of common materials, typical results, and hard stops.

Always run a small test on a scrap from the *same batch* of material. Coatings, adhesives, and dyes change outcomes.

!!! danger "Never laser materials that release chlorine or other highly toxic gases"
    If a material is PVC, vinyl with PVC, or otherwise chlorine-based, **do not cut or engrave it** on a typical diode/CO₂ hobby or small-shop laser. Chlorine-containing fumes can seriously harm you and corrode your machine. When in doubt, identify the plastic first — do not guess.

## Organics that usually work well

| Material | Typical use | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardwood / softwood | Signs, plaques, inlays | Resin and oils vary; watch for pitch flare-ups |
| Plywood / Baltic birch | Cut signs, layered art | Glue lines can char unevenly; exterior glues may be nastier — know your sheet |
| MDF / HDF | Cheap blanks, prototypes | Dusty, strong smell; exhaust is mandatory |
| Paper / cardstock | Invitations, packaging | Fire risk is high — air assist and attention |
| Leather / some vegan leathers | Patches, wallets | Real leather engraves nicely; synthetics vary — some are vinyl (unsafe) |
| Cork | Coasters, boards | Engraves well; cutting thickness limited by power |
| Rubber (laser-safe stamp rubber) | Stamps | Use rubber sold for laser; random rubber may be unsafe |

## Plastics — proceed with knowledge

| Material | Typical result | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cast acrylic (PMMA) | Excellent cut/engrave on CO₂; diodes vary by color | Exhaust acrylic fumes; know cast vs extruded for edge quality |
| Extruded acrylic | Cuts, but edges may differ from cast | Same fume care |
| Delrin / acetal (POM) | Can mark/cut on appropriate lasers | Strong formaldehyde-related concerns — research and vent heavily; many shops avoid it |
| PET / PETG | Sometimes markable | Melting and stringing; test carefully |
| ABS | Often poor / nasty fumes | Many shops avoid lasering ABS |
| Polycarbonate (Lexan, etc.) | Generally a poor laser material | Tends to discolor, absorb poorly, and produce unpleasant results — usually skip |
| Nylon | Unpredictable | Often avoided for cutting |

!!! warning "Polycarbonate"
    Polycarbonate is a frequent “looks like acrylic” trap. It typically does **not** laser like acrylic. Prefer known acrylic sheet from a supplier who labels it.

## Metals

| Situation | What usually happens |
| --- | --- |
| Bare stainless / aluminum with **fiber** | Engrave / anneal / deep mark depending on settings and machine |
| Bare metals with **CO₂** | Poor absorption; reflection risk — not a normal workflow |
| Anodized aluminum | Diode/CO₂/fiber can remove or alter the anodized layer for high-contrast marks |
| Powder-coated / painted metal | Laser often removes coating to reveal metal underneath |
| “Laser marking spray / paste” on metal | Creates a bonded mark with diode/CO₂ on some metals — follow product instructions and cure times |

## Stone, glass, ceramics

| Material | Typical result |
| --- | --- |
| Slate | Excellent high-contrast engrave on many diodes/CO₂ |
| Glass | Surface frost / etch; technique and masking matter; breakage risk |
| Ceramic tile / coated tumblers | Often engrave the coating; substrate varies |
| Marble / granite | Light surface etch possible; depth and contrast vary |

## Dangerous or strongly discouraged materials

Treat this list as **do not laser** unless you have industrial controls and expertise far beyond a typical maker laser:

- **PVC / vinyl / faux leather that is PVC-based** — chlorine gas / HCl risk
- **PVDC and other chlorine plastics**
- **Unknown “mystery plastic”** — identify first (recycling codes help but are not perfect)
- **Polycarbonate** for cutting workflows — poor results and harsh byproducts
- **Fiberglass / carbon fiber composites** — hazardous dust and binders
- **Pressure-treated lumber** — chemical treatments
- **Materials with unknown flame retardants or PTFE coatings** without documentation

!!! danger "Identify before you burn"
    If a client sends “just some plastic,” ask for the material name or a sample Safety Data Sheet (SDS). Refusing an unknown blank is professional, not rude.

## Adhesives, tapes, and laminates

Double-check layers:

- Transfer tape, vinyl application tape, and many wall decals are **vinyl** → unsafe to laser
- Laser plywood with thick glue lines may char or flare
- Laminated engraving plastics (rowmark-style) are often designed for laser — buy products marketed for laser engraving and follow the manufacturer

## Fumes are part of the material choice

Even “safe to laser” materials produce smoke and ultrafine particles. Pair every material decision with [ventilation and safety](../machines/ventilation-and-safety.md). Your lungs and your optics both care.
